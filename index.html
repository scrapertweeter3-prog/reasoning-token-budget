A summarized reasoning trace still bills like a full one

A hidden reasoning trace is an output bill you cannot read. At $15 per million output tokens, a 6,000-token thinking trace costs $0.09 on every call that runs it. Forty calls a day is $3.60, which is $1,314 a year spent on text most interfaces never display.

The summary on screen is not the expense. Two hundred visible tokens cost $0.003 per call, while the trace behind them is thirty times the size and bills at the same output rate. When a provider swaps the full trace for a summary, the meter keeps running on the hidden part, so reasoning transparency is a billing question before it is anything else.

The budget is the lever. Cap the thinking trace at 1,500 tokens on the thirty routine calls of the day and the spend drops by 135,000 tokens daily, $739.12 a year. The ten hard calls keep the full 6,000, so the after-cap total is 105,000 tokens a day, $574.88 a year: a 56 percent cut with the difficult work untouched.

Log the thinking line the way a serving-cost audit logs every other line: requested cap, delivered tokens, rate, per call. A month of that ledger separates calls that think for a living from calls that think out of habit, and the second group is where the cut lands without touching output quality. The summary already tells you what the trace concluded; the ledger tells you what it cost.
